09 — When

Pick a date, work backwards

Most of what goes wrong is a lead time somebody didn't know about. Enter your target date and we'll lay out what has to happen and by when.

    Choosing the date itself

    What the calendar does to you

    Engagement season runs from Thanksgiving through Valentine's Day and takes a large share of the year's proposals. Everyone is together, everyone is off work, and the photos look like the holidays. The cost: photographers book out, restaurants are full, and their family may half-expect it.

    The off-season — March through October — buys you availability, surprise, better light, and venues that aren't crowded. If nothing is forcing December, this is the easier path.

    Golden hour is the hour after sunrise and before sunset. Look up sunset for your exact date, then arrive 90 minutes before — many spots fall into shadow well before the sun is technically down.

    Avoid borrowing a date. Proposing on their birthday or a family anniversary means that day now belongs to two things forever. Some people love that. Ask yourself which kind they are.

    Weekdays are underrated. Quieter venues, empty overlooks, and a Tuesday that becomes memorable on its own.

    If the date is close

    Ready-to-ship settings with a stone already on hand can be done in one to two weeks. If you're tighter than that, propose with a temporary band, an heirloom, or the empty box and design the real ring together afterwards — which many people prefer anyway. Rushing a custom CAD order is how rings arrive wrong.
